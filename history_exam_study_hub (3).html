Chapter 1.1: Paris Peace Conference & Treaty of Versailles

Conducted in 1919 following WWI. The "Big Three" (Clemenceau of France, Lloyd George of Britain, Woodrow Wilson of USA) held opposing aims, resulting in a compromised yet punitive settlement against Germany.

Key Mnemonic: B.R.A.T.S. (Treaty of Versailles Terms)

Chapter 1.2: League of Nations (Structure & Disputes)

Founded in 1920 to maintain collective security. Weakened by US isolationism, lack of a standing army, requirement of unanimous voting in the Assembly, and self-interest of permanent Council members (Britain & France).

Dispute Inspector Matrix (1920s - 1930s):

Successes (1920s)
  • Aaland Islands (1921): Successfully arbitrated between Sweden and Finland.
  • Upper Silesia (1921): Smoothly partitioned between Germany and Poland following plebiscite.
  • Greece vs Bulgaria (1925): League ordered Greek army retreat; Greece complied.
Fatal Failures (1920s-1930s)
  • Corfu Incident (1923): Mussolini defied League; forced Greece to pay compensation directly to Italy.
  • Mukden / Manchuria (1931): Japan ignored Lytton Commission, quit League in 1933.
  • Abyssinian Crisis (1935): Italy invaded; Hoare-Laval Pact exposed British/French hypocrisy; sanctions on oil omitted.

Chapter 2: Weimar Weaknesses & Rise of Nazi Germany

Weimar Structural Weaknesses

  • Proportional Representation: Led to weak, unstable coalition governments (20 different governments in 14 years).
  • Article 48: Allowed President to rule by decree in emergencies, bypassing Reichstag democracy.
  • Economic Disasters: 1923 Hyperinflation (Ruhr Crisis) & 1929 Great Depression (6M unemployed by 1932).

Hitler's Strategic Toolkit

  • Oratorical Charisma & Propaganda: Josef Goebbels orchestrated mass rallies, radio, banners, and scapegoating.
  • 25-Point Programme: Promised work, repeal of Versailles, land, and anti-communist security.
  • Political Backroom Deal: Papen & Hindenburg appointed Hitler Chancellor in Jan 1933 thinking they could control him.

Impact of Nazi Rule (3-Pillar Matrix):

1. Political Control

Enabling Act (Mar 1933): Dictatorial power without Reichstag.

Night of Long Knives (June 1934): SS purged SA leadership (Röhm) & political rivals.

Creation of Führer: Combined President & Chancellor roles after Hindenburg's death.

2. Economic Control

Public Works & RAD: Autobahn construction provided massive employment.

Conscription & Rearmament (1935): Four-Year Plan focused on autarky and war prep.

DAF & KdF: Banned Trade Unions; replaced with German Labour Front & Strength Through Joy.

3. Social Persecution

Youth: Hitler Youth made compulsory (1936); school curricula indoctrinated.

Women: Kitchen, Children, Church (3 Ks); loans for leaving workforce.

Anti-Semitism: 1933 Boycotts → 1935 Nuremberg Laws → 1938 Kristallnacht → Holocaust.

Chapter 3: Militarist & Imperial Japan (1868 - 1941)

Japan's transition from rapid Meiji modernization to aggressive military expansionism driven by constitutional loopholes, economic collapse, and political violence.

1889 Meiji Constitution Flaws

  • Supreme Command: Army & Navy reported directly to Emperor, bypassing Cabinet/Diet.
  • Cabinet Holding: War/Navy Ministers had to be active-duty officers; military could bring down governments by resigning.
  • Economic Triggers: 1923 Great Kanto Earthquake, 1927 Banking Crisis, 1929 Great Depression (US silk tariff ruined farmers).

Military Faction Inspector

Kōdō-ha (Imperial Way Faction): Radical, anti-communist, favoured coup d'état, mystical emperor worship, war with USSR (Feb 26, 1936 Incident failed coup).
Tōsei-ha (Control Faction): Conservative, wanted state control via legal bureaucracy, industrial mobilization, expansion into China (Led by Hideki Tojo).

Chapter 4: Outbreak of WWII in Europe

Hitler's Aggressive Steps (C.R.A.M.C.U.P. Framework)

1933 - 1935
Conscription & Rearmament

Exit League (1933), public conscription & Luftwaffe build-up (1935).

March 1936
Rhineland Remilitarization

Marched 30k troops into demilitarized buffer zone; Allies took no action.

March 1938
Anschluss with Austria

Forced union with Austria; rigged plebiscite (99.75% approval).

Sept 1938
Munich Agreement

Chamberlain ceded Sudetenland; Hitler broke promise and seized rest of Czech in Mar 1939.

Policy of Appeasement Balance Scale Chamberlain's Calculus

Reasons FOR Appeasement:
  • Fear of devastating WWI repeat bombing/casualties.
  • Britain was economically weak and unprepared for war.
  • Belief Versailles was genuinely too harsh on Germany.
  • Hitler viewed as a buffer against Soviet Communism.
Reasons AGAINST (Fatal Flaws):
  • Encouraged Hitler's aggression (proved Allies were weak).
  • Allowed Germany to grow significantly stronger militarily.
  • Alienated USSR, driving Stalin to sign Nazi-Soviet Pact.
  • Betrayed smaller democratic nations (Czechoslovakia).

Chapter 5: Outbreak of WWII in Asia-Pacific

Economic & Resource Pressures

Rapid population explosion (45M in 1900 to 64M in 1930) combined with mountainous, resource-poor geography forced reliance on foreign imports (oil, rubber, iron). Wall Street Crash protectionist tariffs shut off exports.

GEACPS (Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere): Propagandized as "Asia for Asians" to liberate from Western colonialists, but was actually an empire to strip raw resources (Indochina, Malaya, Dutch East Indies).

US-Japan Escalation Spiral to Pearl Harbor

1937: Marco Polo Bridge Incident → Full-scale invasion of China (Nanking Massacre).

1940: Japan occupies Northern French Indochina; US places Embargo on scrap iron and aviation fuel.

July 1941: Japan occupies Southern Indochina; US freezes all Japanese assets & imposes 100% Total Oil Embargo.

Dec 7, 1941: Surprise strike on Pearl Harbor naval fleet to incapacitate US Pacific Fleet before seizing South-East Asian oil fields.
